Introduction & Context

Purpose of this Briefing
This document summarises the current stage of UniVibe and outlines specific areas where collaboration with the Careers and Enterprise team could accelerate a disciplined campus pilot.

What is UniVibe?

UniVibe is a student-only, campus-focused digital marketplace that connects students who need short, practical or creative help with other verified students who can deliver those services for pay. It formalises existing informal work (WhatsApp groups, word-of-mouth, ad hoc favours) into a safer, structured, and employability-aligned system.

Why You Are Receiving This Now

Following our recent conversation, I took additional time to determine the most effective way to present the material so that the level of work completed, and the fit with Careers/Enterprise priorities, is clear. This briefing therefore acts as a focused, web-friendly overview of a larger body of academic and practical work already completed on UniVibe.

Student Validation & Employability Value

Key Validation Signals

The student survey and early prototype testing surfaced several consistent patterns:

  • Students report frequent issues with informal work: delayed payment, non-payment, and unclear expectations.
  • Escrow-style payment and verified profiles are seen as essential, not optional, for in-person and high-trust tasks.
  • A modest monthly subscription is acceptable to providers when it clearly connects to safer work and better visibility.
  • Students strongly value having a documented record of micro-work that they can present as evidence to employers.
\"I already do this kind of work for friends, but there is no way to show it on my CV. If UniVibe can verify and record it, that would actually help me when applying for jobs.\"

The Skills Transcript Concept

Every completed task on UniVibe can be captured as a micro-experience, including task type, feedback, and relevant skills. These entries form a Skills Transcript that sits between a traditional CV and a portfolio, making informal student work visible and legible to employers.

  • Maps naturally to existing graduate attribute frameworks.
  • Supports reflective practice and evidence-based applications.
  • Provides the university with anonymised, aggregate insight into student skill development and activity.

Alignment with Careers & Enterprise Priorities

  • Employability: Converts informal work into evidence that can be used in applications, interviews, and careers guidance sessions.
  • Enterprise: Encourages students to treat their skills as services, developing pricing, communication, and client management capability.
  • Data & Insight: Offers a route to understanding what kinds of skills students are monetising and where gaps exist.

Financial & Operational Readiness

Headline Financial View (Pilot-Ready Stage)

  • Revenue Model: Provider subscription (e.g. £4.99/month) plus commission on completed tasks, with potential institutional licence for analytics/dashboard.
  • Break-even: Operating break-even forecast within the first 12 months at relatively modest penetration of the Salford student base.
  • Capital Efficiency: Lean cost base, limited fixed overheads, and staged use of external funding.

What Exists Today

  • Structured financial forecasting for a Salford-first launch (cash flow, break-even, and risk scenarios).
  • Clarity on demand assumptions (user growth, transaction frequency, and pricing).
  • Preliminary thinking on funding sources (grants, competitions, and later-stage investment) that could complement university support.

Operational Building Blocks in Place

  • Verification-first account creation flow (students only).
  • Task posting, discovery, booking, and escrow-style payment logic.
  • Skills Transcript structure aligned with employability objectives.
  • Initial risk and safeguarding considerations (e.g. hour limits, dispute handling).

Partnership Opportunities with Careers & Enterprise

Summary
UniVibe does not need help to "define an idea"; it needs institutional partners to help design and deliver a safe, evidence-led campus pilot that advances student employability and enterprise outcomes.

Where Careers & Enterprise Can Add Most Value

Area What Exists Potential Role for Careers/Enterprise
Pilot Design Pilot parameters drafted (scope, campus-only focus, KPIs). Co-design a 6—12 month Salford pilot, including student cohorts, key milestones, and evaluation criteria.
Student Recruitment Concept for "anchor providers" and early adopters. Promote to entrepreneurial students and societies; identify 20—30 initial service providers and a similar number of service users.
Employability Integration Skills Transcript and micro-credential structures drafted. Align transcripts and micro-credentials with Salford's employability and graduate attribute frameworks; explore recognition in awards or schemes.
Verification & Compliance Verification-first logic; consideration of hour limits and safeguarding. Advise on policy alignment, signpost to relevant university teams, and explore technical routes for enrolment/ID verification.
Data & Analytics Concept for institutional dashboards. Co-define metrics that would be most useful for Careers/Enterprise (e.g. skills developed, hours worked, types of activity).
Funding & Visibility Initial mapping of grant and competition options. Signpost relevant internal funds or competitions; provide letters of support or case study input where appropriate.

Proposed Next Steps

  3. Co-design a lightweight pilot plan (timelines, cohorts, and success metrics).
